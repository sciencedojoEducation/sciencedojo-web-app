## Fcode Labs: commercial lessons to apply

1. Sell responsibility for a concrete business problem and a defined engineering workstream. Make the buyer, problem and deliverable clear before listing technologies.
2. Begin with a bounded paid discovery, pilot or feature where appropriate; earn ongoing development and support through reliable delivery and accumulated product knowledge.
3. Build trust through relevant working examples, clear communication, accurate personal contributions and permission to show work. Label in-house products, demonstrations and previous-employer experience correctly.
4. Treat delivery continuity and relationships as strengths. A small team can offer direct access to the people doing the work.
5. Match commitments to actual available capacity and full delivery costs, including coordination, review, sales and support. Do not infer sustainable margins or validated rates from competitor directories or subscription labels.
6. Keep agency partnerships as a possible acquisition route alongside direct clients. Previous discussion of agencies does not supersede the backlog's direct-client-first decision or authorize outreach.
7. Develop repeatable methods and reusable components where real projects justify them. A subscription alone does not create software-like economics.

## Zero Gravity: visual and UX lessons to apply

1. **Design one coherent system.** Typography, spacing, colour, graphic language, shapes, imagery and motion should reinforce the same identity.
2. **Give hierarchy a clear order.** Deliberate headline scale, weight and line breaks should lead into a short explanation and an obvious next action. Avoid making every element equally prominent.
3. **Assign colour a role.** Use a calm neutral foundation, consistent emphasis and a recognisable action colour. Softer supporting colours need rules rather than arbitrary distribution.
4. **Create rhythm through composition.** Vary section layouts and density while maintaining alignment and shared visual rules. Avoid repeating the same card arrangement throughout the page.
5. **Make graphics specific to the brand.** Purposeful illustration can make the identity recognisable; generic workspace photography contributes less to a distinctive team story.
6. **Start with recognisable customer problems.** Then explain the service, show relevant proof and make the next step understandable.
7. **Use interaction to add depth.** Keep essential meaning visible. Simulated typing, hidden service details and conversation styling can add waiting or ambiguity; do not imply live support where there is none.
8. **Support buying decisions with concrete work.** A memorable broad promise and client logos need specific examples of the problem, contribution and supported result.
9. **Compose mobile deliberately.** Adapt navigation and content patterns, preserve readable type and comfortable controls, and avoid excessive hero whitespace or long nested disclosures. Desktop polish alone is insufficient.
10. **Verify the actual implementation.** Check responsive layouts, focus and keyboard use, reduced motion, readable contrast and working enquiry paths. Concept images do not demonstrate these behaviours.

### Strategic architecture

The principal interpretation is that Fcode supports supplier evaluation, while Zero Gravity tells a more concentrated brand and learning-performance story. Their strongest reusable lesson is to separate **problem**, **solution**, **engagement model**, **proof** and **product**. These answer different questions and should not become one flat list of services.

For ScienceDojo, the homepage should let a buyer recognise a situation, understand the relevant intervention, inspect actual work and know the next step. Additional routes can answer technical reviewers' questions and explain the team. A small company does not need to reproduce the full navigation or catalogue of a larger supplier.

### Proposed original interaction: a problem-to-solution demonstrator

Build one carefully scoped illustrative interaction if implementation is later requested. The default state must already explain a complete example. Three plain selectors could represent **Simplify a workflow**, **Improve an application**, and **Connect systems**. A selection updates one compact visual, a concrete explanation, a deliverable example and a matching enquiry action together. It should not require visitors to know a software category before recognising their problem.

For an integration example, depict a synthetic request entering once, receiving validation and updating a second system. Show pending, successful and recoverable-error states, so the demonstration communicates engineering thoughtfulness rather than an attractive line connecting boxes. Avoid measured time-saving numbers until supported. Call it an illustrative example, not a client project. It should work through click, touch and keyboard; retain all meaning with reduced motion and a static fallback; pause decorative activity outside view. Do not use real accounts, private learning data or live external integrations for this sales demonstration.

### Original ScienceDojo design requirements proposed from the study

- **Architecture:** a short problem-led engineering homepage, focused work details, approach, People and enquiry. The existing For Businesses/Learning Design offer remains intact. Keep the registered company identity clear and reuse the approved lowercase wordmark.
- **Opening:** specific situation, clear engineering intervention, useful visual and direct action. No portrait strip or team card on the homepage. Sri Lankan identity can appear in a short supporting statement.
- **Buyer routes:** use three understandable problem groups. Link to deeper scope information and a real relevant example; distinguish an advisory task, defined build and separately scoped continuation only where the team can offer them.
- **Proof:** replace all fake/generated dashboard content with a real safe product flow, an honestly labelled synthetic demonstration or a permission-cleared previous contribution. Use screenshot captions that explain the task rather than merely naming the product.
- **Typography:** choose one primary family, controlled weight and size hierarchy, comfortable body measure and deliberate responsive line breaks. Tentative design ranges are 64–80px desktop headline, 36–44px phone headline, 17–19px main body; these are proposals requiring actual layout checks, not reference-site requirements.
- **Colour:** near-white/cream foundation, navy text, one teal action colour, light sage/lavender/peach only where they have a clear supporting role. Avoid arbitrary section colours, excessive accent use and contrast sacrificed for subtlety.
- **Geometry:** a shared spacing scale, aligned edges, restrained corners and line weights, consistent buttons and icon style. Use separators for related content; do not box everything. Decorative headline underlines remain excluded.
- **Motion:** retain one explanatory interaction and restrained state changes. Do not animate every heading, loop every section, hijack scroll, obscure the cursor or make a loader delay the content. Reduced motion and no-animation states must be complete.
- **Mobile:** one-column narrative, compact hero, obvious primary action, usable disclosure and appropriately composed work previews. People content stays on its own route. The same buyer questions should be answered without desktop-only interactions.
- **Contact:** name, email and a short useful description; organisation/timing optional unless needed for the actual offer. Show selected interest explicitly. Include direct email and what happens next. Verify labels, inline errors, preserved input, honest success, duplicate handling and actual inbox receipt when implemented.
- **Robustness:** verify keyboard/focus, menu dismissal, reduced motion, zoom, contrast, layout shifts, slow loading and the chosen real enquiry delivery path. Visual elegance must survive loading, selected, empty, error and confirmation states. No such future checks are marked passed by this review.

### Meaning of advanced + simple

**Advanced** means a coherent original visual system, relevant evidence, thoughtful interactions, clear delivery information and technically careful implementation. **Simple** means buyers immediately understand the problem being addressed, can inspect proof without hunting, can skip interaction and can start a conversation without unnecessary effort. The proposed demonstrator should be the signature feature; technical depth and personalities can be reached through direct routes. Additional effects must earn their place by improving understanding, orientation or control.
