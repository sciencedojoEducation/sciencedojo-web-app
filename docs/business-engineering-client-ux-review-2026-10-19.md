# Engineering page — client comprehension and visual UX review

19 October 2026. A focused follow-through to the [eight-company study and complete page audit](business-engineering-company-audit-2026-10-19.md). Scope: the existing local engineering page, its aesthetics, graphics, interaction cues and the questions a prospective client needs answered. This is an expert review supported by published UX research and local browser evidence; no prospective client was interviewed or observed.

## Finding

The visual direction is appropriate: structured typography, restrained blue/navy/cyan, generous spacing, inspectable product interfaces and a friendly lower team section. The remaining design opportunity is to make the page easier to interpret. Clients should not have to translate abstract headings, guess whether a control navigates elsewhere or invent a technical brief before contacting Piumal.

Our working hypothesis is that clearer expectations and credible evidence will help suitable visitors judge fit. It is not a measured claim about trust, persuasion or conversion. Attractive graphics cannot substitute for relevant delivery evidence, a realistic scope or clear commercial arrangements.

## Evidence and method

The earlier supplier study covers thoughtbot, Netguru, 10up, Fcode Labs, 99x, Atomic Object, Thoughtworks and Electric. It identifies a useful presentation sequence: fit, contribution, evidence, engagement and next action. This review adds source inspection of the local components, baseline production screenshots and a focused UX literature review. The table below separates published principles from our application.

| Source | Relevant principle | Application and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NN/G: aesthetic-usability effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/) | Visual appeal can influence perceived ease and hide usability problems in subjective feedback. | Preserve visual care, but check tasks, states and comprehension separately from asking whether the page looks good. The effect does not establish that our chosen style will sell engineering work. |
| [NN/G: layer-cake scanning](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) | Descriptive, visually distinct headings help readers locate relevant information. | Replace abstract section headlines with labels about the product, handover, people and costs. Large type remains subordinate to useful meaning. |
| [NN/G: information scent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/) | Labels and surrounding cues help people predict where an action leads. | Use selection/check cues for an on-page example. Retain external-style arrows only where the surrounding action actually suggests a destination or next step. This is a design judgment, not observed visitor confusion. |
| [NN/G: recognition and recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/) | Visible options and context reduce reliance on remembering or generating information. | Pair recognisable desired changes with explicit service categories, and give short persistent brief guidance. No claim is made that the options cover every client's need. |
| [NN/G: progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) | Keep the main task clear while making secondary detail available when needed. | Keep service scope visible; retain optional detailed synthetic exercises, commercial FAQs and a fictional brief example. Essential fit or commitments must not be hidden inside a demo. |
| [Stanford: web credibility guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html) | Verifiable material, identifiable people, contact details and appropriate professional presentation support credibility. | Retain actual in-house evidence, accurate roles, registered company prose and email. This is a 2002 research summary, not contemporary validation of this particular B2B page. No invented logos, statistics, credentials or hobbies. |
| [W3C WAI: form instructions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/instructions/) | Persistent instructions and associated descriptions explain what information is required. | Explain organisation and the short brief next to their fields; preserve required/optional wording and accessible error associations. This does not certify the complete site for accessibility. |

Sources reviewed on 19 October 2026. These principles inform hypotheses; they do not reveal the psychology of our actual buyers. No colour-to-emotion rule, urgency tactic or numerical conversion forecast is used.

## Aesthetics, graphics and interaction decisions

**Hierarchy and reading rhythm.** Keep the large three-line opening proposition, compact navigation and one dominant contact action. The quieter secondary action leads to evidence. Alternate light product proof with the navy operations section to distinguish chapters without increasing the palette. The revised headings name their content: “Inside our learning platform”, “From your first brief to a clear handover”, and “Costs, handover and next steps”. The service section asks what would make work easier. These are scannable signposts, not claims that every project includes a learning platform.

**Graphic meaning.** The opening's five interface blocks previously had two elements outside the connecting route. The SVG now passes continuously through website, enquiry, course, progress and the final check. Its visible caption describes a useful next step and retains the concept label. It remains an illustrative journey, not a promise that all client projects need these five stages or evidence of a live integration. Actual sample-data screenshots continue to carry the proof role. Supporting workflow/studio art and matching team cartoons remain distinct from product evidence.

**Control expectations.** The three main service choices now say “Make our website clearer”, “Improve our application” and “Connect our everyday tools”, with the service category underneath. An arrow invites selection and a check identifies the active choice; `aria-pressed` exposes the same state. On phones the unselected arrow points down toward the example. Selection continues to update the synthetic example and editable enquiry interest without navigating. Detailed exercises remain optional and cancel pending work when closed or reset.

**Warmth with structure.** Keep the illustrated team lower on the page and use confirmed responsibilities rather than invented personality. Name Piumal beside the contact steps so visitors can see who receives the brief without scrolling back to the team. The small first-step sentence under the hero actions sets a proportionate expectation without advertising free work, instant availability or a response deadline.

**Form effort and reassurance.** The organisation field remains required under the existing storage contract, with a persistent explanation that a business, team or project name is suitable. The goal guidance asks for a few sentences about the current situation, desired change and tools, retaining the instruction to omit confidential material. Labels stay visible. The same hint remains associated with the field when an error is added. No extra qualification fields, calendar gate, new tracking or live-chat interruption are introduced.

**Motion and restraint.** Keep the existing bounded animation, manual pause, live reduced-motion response and offscreen/background pause. No additional animation is required to explain these changes. Basic meaning stays available without scripts, hover or motion. No underlined decorative headline, flashing urgency badge, automatic modal or new runtime dependency is added.

## What remains more valuable than another redesign

1. A permission-backed external engineering case with the actual need, contribution and inspectable result. Current in-house proof is useful, but it is not a client testimonial.
2. Real discovery conversations showing which problems people recognise, what they find unclear and whether the proposed first scope fits. The page cannot establish demand by itself.
3. Measured capacity and delivery effort before publishing engineering prices or turnaround promises. Existing learning price/scope and paid-work permission remain unchanged.
4. Independent comprehension and intended-device checks. Automated browser acceptance establishes specified behaviour, not human understanding or psychological confidence.

## Small human review to run next

Recruit several plausible buyers outside the project, preferably people who arrange training, business websites or internal applications. Use a fictional task and no private account data. Show the opening briefly and ask “What does this team help with?” Then let them explore without coaching: “Find something relevant to this situation”, “Show me what they have actually built”, “What would you expect after contacting them?” and “Prepare a short fictional enquiry without sending it.” Ask what remains uncertain before asking for aesthetic preferences.

Record actual words, chosen paths, hesitation and mistaken expectations; distinguish observed difficulty from facilitator interpretation. Check whether people confuse concept art with a real product or a demo with a purchased service. With a small exploratory sample, report individual findings rather than a conversion percentage or population-level result. No recruitment, outreach or human test has been performed by this implementation.
