# German A1 adult course: curriculum and exam bridge

This is the authoring map for the 15 shared chapters in `deutsch-a1-komplett`. Every chapter contains transcript-backed listening, functional reading, saved writing, recorded speaking, vocabulary with images, and a check. Grammar is taught in the situation where learners need it. The exam routes are independent practice, not official Goethe or telc certification.

In the longer first three chapters, the short mini-checks are formative and repeatable, not completion gates. The designated listening/reading checks, chapter checks, and saved writing/speaking portfolio tasks remain required. This keeps the detailed beginner practice available without making every small question a condition for moving on.

Chapters 1–3 now end with an original, unseen short text and a non-blocking exam-transfer question before the chapter recap. Chapters 2–14 begin with one optional recall question from the preceding chapter, so learners retrieve earlier language before moving into the next everyday situation. The recall options rotate their correct position; these questions are practice, not a proficiency grade.

The pictured deck currently has 623 unique target words and expressions across the shared chapters, with no repeated picture card within a chapter. Stand-alone grammar labels such as *Nominativ*, *Artikel* and *Verbstamm* are not counted as picture vocabulary; their useful patterns remain in contextual explanations and examples.

Each chapter shows its situation-specific core picture deck directly. Inherited word banks are labeled by topic and collapsed as optional decks, rather than placing up to 130 cards in the main path of a single chapter. They remain available for recognition and later review without becoming completion gates.

The shared 40-question check now has ten newly recorded listening situations rather than replaying lesson dialogues. They cover names, family, time, food, services, travel, work, social plans, health and appointments. Each recording has an optional transcript for accessibility; using it changes the result from a pure listening measure to a reading-supported one. The remaining objective questions sample reading, everyday expressions and grammar in context. Writing and speaking portfolios remain self-assessed, not part of the objective score.

The [version-controlled coverage matrix](../lib/german-a1-coverage.ts) is checked by automated tests. It maps the areas in the [Goethe A1 vocabulary specification](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/de/A1_SD1_Wortliste_02.pdf) to Chapters 1–14, so Chapter 15 is review rather than the first encounter with any area.

Coverage is backed by learner activities, not just labels: Chapter 3 includes a person-and-profession card; Chapter 8 includes a post-office pickup notice; Chapter 9 includes a hotel notice and luggage question; Chapter 11 adds an internet/TV film-night message; Chapter 12 adds a pharmacy notice and useful questions; Chapter 13 teaches the four seasons through a nature outing and rain alternative; Chapter 14 includes a course-registration form to read and fill in. Each of these secondary areas has a required comprehension check.

After the shared final check, the learner chooses either a Goethe or telc route. Each has orientation, Hören, Lesen, Schreiben, Sprechen, a short original simulation plus a linked official full model, and performance review. The two general Start Deutsch 1 exams have a shared origin and similar task families; the separate routes focus on the learner's chosen provider and its official practice set, timing and answer sheet rather than inventing different A1 grammar. The short simulations have route-specific listening recordings and questions that are new to the learner, rather than repeating the earlier Hören drill. Their three listening situations are combined into one player, so the listening strategy explains the approximation to official replay counts. The source of truth for timings and current task formats is the [Goethe practice material](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/prf/prf/gzsd1/ueb.html) or the [telc exam page](https://www.telc.net/en/language-examinations/certificate-exams/german/start-german1-telc-german-a1/). Third-party questions and audio are linked, not copied.
